Q: A 45-year-old has a slow-growing, painless, firm swelling in front of and below the ear lobule for 5 years, with normal facial nerve function. FNAC shows epithelial and myoepithelial cells in a chondromyxoid stroma. The treatment is:
A) Enucleation
B) Superficial parotidectomy with facial nerve preservation
C) Total radical parotidectomy
D) Radiotherapy
ANS: B
TAGS: PQ, Clinical
EX: **Pleomorphic adenoma** is the most common salivary tumour, usually in the superficial lobe of the parotid. Treatment is **superficial (or extracapsular) parotidectomy**; enucleation leaves pseudopod extensions behind and causes recurrence. B) Auriculotemporal nerve
C) Facial nerve
D) Lingual nerve

TAGS: PQ, Eponym
EX: **Frey's syndrome (gustatory sweating)**: post-ganglionic parasympathetic secretomotor fibres of the **auriculotemporal nerve** regrow to supply sweat glands and skin vessels. It is demonstrated by Minor's starch-iodine test and treated with botulinum toxin injection.

Q: Salivary calculi are most common in the:
A) Parotid gland
B) Submandibular gland
C) Sublingual gland
D) Minor salivary glands
ANS: B
TAGS: One-liner
EX: About 80% of salivary stones occur in the **submandibular gland** because its secretion is more mucinous and alkaline with a higher calcium content, and Wharton's duct runs uphill. Most are radio-opaque. Nerves at risk during gland excision: marginal mandibular, lingual and hypoglossal.

Q: A 20-year-old has a smooth cystic swelling at the anterior border of the upper third of the sternocleidomastoid. Aspirate contains cholesterol crystals. The swelling arises from the:

B) Second branchial cleft
C) Thyroglossal duct

Q: A newborn has a soft, brilliantly transilluminant, partially compressible swelling in the posterior triangle of the neck. The diagnosis is:

C) Sternomastoid tumour
D) Dermoid cyst

Q: A carotid body tumour characteristically:
A) Moves vertically but not horizontally
B) Moves horizontally but not vertically
C) Is brilliantly transilluminant
D) Should be confirmed by FNAC
ANS: B

It is a paraganglioma (chemodectoma); the Shamblin classification grades vessel involvement. Biopsy and FNAC are avoided because of bleeding.

B) Lumbar puncture for xanthochromia

Q: Cushing's triad of raised intracranial pressure consists of:
A) Hypotension, tachycardia, tachypnoea
B) Hypertension, bradycardia, irregular respiration
C) Hypertension, tachycardia, fever
D) Hypotension, bradycardia, apnoea
ANS: B
TAGS: Eponym
EX: **Cushing's triad**: hypertension (with widened pulse pressure), bradycardia and irregular respiration; it is a late sign of impending herniation.

Q: An elderly man with cervical spondylosis falls on his face and has weakness that is much greater in the arms than in the legs. The diagnosis is:

Q: The most common soft tissue sarcoma in children is:
A) Liposarcoma
B) Rhabdomyosarcoma
C) Synovial sarcoma
D) Fibrosarcoma
ANS: B
TAGS: One-liner
EX: **Rhabdomyosarcoma** is the most common in children (head and neck, genitourinary). In adults, liposarcoma is the most common in the retroperitoneum and undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma in the extremities. Sarcomas spread by blood to the **lungs**; **histological grade** is the most important prognostic factor.
PEARL: Sarcomas that spread to lymph nodes (SCARE): Synovial sarcoma, Clear cell sarcoma, Angiosarcoma, Rhabdomyosarcoma, Epithelioid sarcoma. Biopsy is by core needle, or an incisional biopsy placed along the line of the future resection.

Q: The pectoralis major myocutaneous flap, the workhorse flap for head and neck reconstruction, is based on the:

Q: In unilateral cleft lip, the repair is traditionally timed by the 'rule of tens'. Which is NOT part of it?

Q: A patient has a finger held in flexion with uniform fusiform swelling, tenderness along the flexor sheath and severe pain on passive extension. The diagnosis is:
A) Paronychia
B) Felon
C) Suppurative flexor tenosynovitis
D) Web space infection
ANS: C
TAGS: Eponym
EX: **Kanavel's four signs**: flexed posture of the finger, fusiform swelling, tenderness along the flexor tendon sheath, and pain on passive extension (the earliest and most reliable). Treatment is urgent sheath drainage and irrigation. The most common hand infection overall is paronychia.

Q: An ulcer with an undermined edge is characteristic of:
A) Basal cell carcinoma
B) Tuberculous ulcer
C) Squamous cell carcinoma
D) Venous ulcer
ANS: B
TAGS: One-liner
EX: Undermined edge: **tuberculosis**. Punched-out edge: syphilitic (gummatous), trophic and arterial ulcers. Rolled (beaded) edge: basal cell carcinoma. Everted edge: squamous cell carcinoma. Sloping edge: healing or venous ulcer.

Q: A 3-month-old infant has a rapidly enlarging bright red, raised lesion on the cheek that was not present at birth. The first-line treatment, if treatment is needed, is:
A) Surgical excision
B) Oral propranolol
C) Pulsed dye laser
D) Intralesional steroids
ANS: B
TAGS: Management
EX: **Infantile haemangioma** appears after birth, proliferates in the first year and then involutes (most by 5 to 7 years; GLUT-1 positive). Most need only observation; **oral propranolol** is first-line for lesions that threaten vision, airway or function, or ulcerate. Vascular malformations (for example, port-wine stain) are present at birth and never involute.

Q: Under the Transplantation of Human Organs Act in India, brain-stem death is certified by a board of doctors performing the tests:
A) Once, by two doctors
B) Twice, 6 hours apart, by a board of four doctors
C) Three times, 24 hours apart
D) Twice, 1 hour apart, by any two doctors
ANS: B
TAGS: PQ, One-liner
EX: The board has **four members**: the medical administrator in charge of the hospital, an authorised specialist, a neurologist or neurosurgeon, and the treating doctor. Brain-stem tests, including the apnoea test, are done **twice, at least 6 hours apart**.

Q: The most important opportunistic viral infection between 1 and 6 months after solid organ transplantation is:
A) Herpes simplex virus
B) Cytomegalovirus
C) Epstein-Barr virus
D) Hepatitis B virus
ANS: B
TAGS: One-liner
EX: **CMV** is the most common significant infection in this period, with highest risk when a seronegative recipient receives a seropositive organ. Prophylaxis and treatment use valganciclovir or ganciclovir. EBV causes post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder; BK virus causes nephropathy after renal transplant.

Q: The maximum acceptable cold ischaemia time is shortest for which organ?
A) Kidney
B) Liver
C) Heart
D) Pancreas
ANS: C
TAGS: One-liner
EX: Approximate limits: **heart 4 to 6 hours**, lung 6 to 8 hours, liver 12 hours, pancreas 12 to 18 hours, kidney 24 to 36 hours. University of Wisconsin solution is the standard preservation fluid for abdominal organs.

Q: A graft exchanged between genetically identical twins is called:
A) Autograft
B) Isograft
C) Allograft
D) Xenograft
ANS: B
TAGS: One-liner
EX: Autograft: same individual. **Isograft (syngeneic)**: genetically identical individuals. Allograft (homograft): different individuals of the same species. Xenograft (heterograft): different species.

Q: The principal dose-limiting toxicity of tacrolimus is:
A) Bone marrow suppression
B) Nephrotoxicity
C) Pulmonary fibrosis
D) Haemorrhagic cystitis
ANS: B
TAGS: Pharmacology
EX: **Calcineurin inhibitors** (tacrolimus, ciclosporin) cause **nephrotoxicity**, hypertension, neurotoxicity (tremor) and post-transplant diabetes (tacrolimus more). Ciclosporin also causes gum hypertrophy and hirsutism. Mycophenolate causes gastrointestinal upset and marrow suppression; sirolimus impairs wound healing and causes hyperlipidaemia.
