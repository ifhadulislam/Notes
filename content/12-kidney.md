# Kidney & Urinary Tract

## Renal Structure & Function — Basics

- Glomerular filtration barrier : ==fenestrated endothelium → GBM (type IV collagen, laminin, heparan sulfate — negative charge) → podocyte foot processes with slit diaphragm (nephrin — NPHS1, podocin — NPHS2)==.
- ==Mesangial cells== : contractile, phagocytic, secrete matrix; ==site of IgA deposits==.
- ==Juxtaglomerular apparatus== : JG cells (renin) + ==macula densa== (DCT) + lacis cells.

### Urine analysis — key findings :

| Finding | Significance |
|---|---|
| ==RBC casts== | ==Glomerulonephritis (nephritic)== |
| ==Dysmorphic RBCs (acanthocytes)== | ==Glomerular hematuria== |
| ==WBC casts== | ==Pyelonephritis==, interstitial nephritis |
| ==Fatty casts, oval fat bodies ("Maltese cross" under polarized light)== | ==Nephrotic syndrome== |
| ==Muddy brown granular casts== | ==Acute tubular necrosis== |
| ==Waxy (broad) casts== | ==Advanced chronic renal failure== |
| Hyaline casts | Normal / concentrated urine (Tamm–Horsfall protein — ==uromodulin==) |
| Eosinophiluria | ==Acute interstitial nephritis (drug)== |
| ==Bence Jones protein== | Myeloma (not detected by dipstick) |

### Nephritic vs nephrotic :

| Feature | ==Nephritic syndrome== | ==Nephrotic syndrome== |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | ==Inflammation (proliferation) → GBM rupture== | ==Podocyte injury → ↑ permeability== |
| Proteinuria | < 3.5 g/day (subnephrotic) | ==> 3.5 g/day (adult); > 40 mg/m²/h (child)== |
| Hematuria | ==Present (RBC casts)== | Usually absent |
| Edema | Mild (periorbital) | ==Massive, generalized (anasarca)== |
| Hypertension | ==Present== | Usually absent |
| Others | ==Oliguria, azotemia== | ==Hypoalbuminemia, hyperlipidemia, lipiduria, hypercoagulability (↓ ATIII — renal vein thrombosis)==, infections (↓ IgG) |

### Mechanisms of glomerular injury :

1. ==In situ immune complex== — intrinsic antigen (==anti-GBM — linear deposits==; ==PLA2R in membranous==) or planted antigen
2. ==Circulating immune complex deposition== — ==granular (lumpy-bumpy)== deposits (SLE, PSGN, IgA)
3. ==Cell-mediated (T cells)== and ==complement alternative pathway (C3G, aHUS)==
4. ==Podocyte injury== — toxins, antibodies, genetic (nephrin, podocin, ==APOL1==)
5. Nephron loss → ==secondary FSGS (hyperfiltration)==

## Nephritic Syndrome — Primary Diseases

| Disease | Key points | LM | IF | EM |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ==Post-streptococcal GN (PSGN) / acute proliferative== | Children; ==1–4 weeks after group A β-hemolytic streptococcal pharyngitis (or 3–6 weeks after skin — impetigo)==; ==nephritogenic strains (types 12, 4, 1)==; antigen : ==SpeB, NAPlr==; ==↑ ASO, anti-DNase B==, ==↓ C3 (normalizes in 6–8 weeks)==; ==95% children recover== | ==Enlarged, hypercellular glomeruli== — ==proliferation + neutrophils== | ==Granular "lumpy-bumpy" IgG, C3== (starry sky) | ==Subepithelial "humps"== |
| ==IgA nephropathy (Berger disease)== | ==Most common GN worldwide==; ==recurrent gross hematuria 1–2 days after mucosal (URTI/GI) infection== ("synpharyngitic"); ==galactose-deficient IgA1== + anti-glycan antibodies; ==↑ in celiac, liver disease (cirrhosis — ↓ IgA clearance)==, HSP (systemic counterpart); ==normal C3== | ==Mesangial proliferation== | ==Mesangial IgA (± C3)== — diagnostic | ==Mesangial deposits== |
| ==Alport syndrome (hereditary nephritis)== | ==Type IV collagen α5 (COL4A5 — X-linked, most common)==, α3/α4 (AR); ==hematuria + sensorineural deafness + ocular (anterior lenticonus)== ; "Can't see, can't pee, can't hear high C" | Non-specific; foam cells | Negative (absent α5 staining) | ==GBM thinning, splitting & lamination — "basket-weave" appearance== |
| ==Thin basement membrane disease== | ==Benign familial hematuria==; COL4A3/A4 heterozygous | Normal | Negative | ==Diffuse thinning of GBM== |

### Rapidly progressive GN (RPGN — crescentic) :

- Clinical : ==rapid loss of renal function (days–weeks)==; histology : ==crescents in > 50% glomeruli==.
- ==Crescents== = proliferation of ==parietal epithelial cells + monocytes/macrophages + fibrin== in Bowman space (fibrin is the stimulus).

| Type | Mechanism | IF | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| ==Type I — anti-GBM== | ==Antibodies against α3 chain (NC1 domain) of type IV collagen== | ==Linear IgG + C3== | ==Goodpasture syndrome (+ pulmonary hemorrhage — HLA-DRB1*1501)==, renal-limited anti-GBM |
| ==Type II — immune complex== | Any immune-complex GN | ==Granular== | ==PSGN, lupus, IgA, HSP== |
| ==Type III — pauci-immune== | ==ANCA-associated== (most common type in adults) | ==Little / no deposits== | ==GPA (c-ANCA), MPA (p-ANCA), EGPA==, idiopathic |

## Secondary (Systemic) Glomerular Disease

| Disease | Key renal pathology |
|---|---|
| ==Lupus nephritis== | ==Class IV diffuse proliferative (most common/severe) — wire-loop (subendothelial), full-house IF==; class V membranous (nephrotic); hematoxylin bodies; see Immunopathology |
| ==Diabetic nephropathy== | ==Most common cause of ESRD==; ==earliest : GBM thickening (EM)== & ==microalbuminuria (30–300 mg/day)==; ==diffuse mesangial sclerosis (most common)==; ==nodular glomerulosclerosis — Kimmelstiel–Wilson nodules (PAS +, specific)==; ==hyaline arteriolosclerosis of both afferent AND efferent arterioles==; ==fibrin cap, capsular drop==; ==Armanni–Ebstein lesion (glycogen in PCT)==; ==papillary necrosis==, pyelonephritis; nonenzymatic glycation (AGEs) |
| ==Renal amyloidosis== | ==Mesangial & subendothelial deposits, Congo red + apple-green birefringence==; nephrotic; large pale kidneys (even in ESRD) |
| ==HSP (IgA vasculitis)== | ==Mesangial IgA== ; purpura, arthralgia, abdominal pain — children |
| ==PAN== | Renal infarcts (medium arteries), ==no GN== |
| ==Microscopic polyangiitis / GPA== | ==Pauci-immune necrotizing crescentic GN== |
| HIV-associated nephropathy | ==Collapsing FSGS==, ==microcystic tubular dilation==, ==tubuloreticular inclusions (EM — interferon)== |
| Light chain cast nephropathy (myeloma kidney) | ==Fractured, waxy, "hard" casts surrounded by multinucleate giant cells== in distal tubules; ==light chain proteinuria==; also amyloid, LCDD, hypercalcemia |
| Sickle cell nephropathy | Papillary necrosis, ==hyposthenuria (inability to concentrate — earliest)==, FSGS |
| Preeclampsia | ==Glomerular endotheliosis (swollen endothelium, bloodless glomeruli)== |

:::flow Diabetic nephropathy — progression
Hyperfiltration (↑ GFR, enlarged kidneys)
==GBM thickening + mesangial expansion== (structural)
==Microalbuminuria== (earliest clinical marker — ACEi/ARB, SGLT2i protective)
==Overt proteinuria / nephrotic syndrome==, Kimmelstiel–Wilson nodules
↓ GFR → ==ESRD==
:::

## Tubular & Interstitial Diseases

### Acute tubular injury / necrosis (ATN) :

- ==Most common cause of acute kidney injury (AKI)==.

| Type | Causes | Morphology |
|---|---|---|
| ==Ischemic ATN== | ==Hypotension, shock (sepsis, hemorrhage), burns==, surgery | ==Patchy (skip) necrosis== along nephron — ==straight portion of PCT (S3) and thick ascending limb==; ==tubulorrhexis (basement membrane disruption)==; ==Tamm–Horsfall casts== in distal tubules |
| ==Nephrotoxic ATN== | ==Aminoglycosides, amphotericin B, radiocontrast, cisplatin, heavy metals (mercury), ethylene glycol (oxalate crystals — ballooning), myoglobin (rhabdomyolysis), hemoglobin, uric acid== | ==Diffuse, extensive necrosis of PCT== with ==basement membrane intact== |

:::flow Clinical course of ATN
==Initiation phase== (~36 h) : precipitating event, slight ↓ urine output
==Maintenance phase== : ==oliguria (40–400 mL/day)==, ==hyperkalemia==, metabolic acidosis, uremia, fluid overload
==Recovery phase== : ==polyuria (large volumes)== → ==hypokalemia== & infections — 50% deaths during this phase historically
:::

- ==Muddy brown granular casts==, ==FENa > 2%== (vs prerenal < 1%).

### Acute cortical necrosis :

- ==Bilateral, symmetric ischemic necrosis of the cortex (medulla & juxtamedullary spared)== — ==obstetric emergencies (abruptio placentae — most common), septic shock, HUS, DIC==; microvascular thrombosis + vasospasm → ==anuria==; ==irreversible==; =="tram-track" cortical calcification== on X-ray.

### Pyelonephritis :

| Feature | ==Acute pyelonephritis== | ==Chronic pyelonephritis== |
|---|---|---|
| Route | ==Ascending (most common — E. coli)==; hematogenous (S. aureus, TB) | ==Chronic obstructive== or ==reflux nephropathy (VUR)== |
| Morphology | ==Patchy interstitial suppurative inflammation, intratubular neutrophil aggregates, WBC casts==; ==tubular necrosis==; spares glomeruli | ==Coarse, irregular, asymmetric corticomedullary scars (U-shaped) over dilated, blunted, deformed calyces==; ==polar scars (reflux — upper & lower poles)== |
| Microscopy | Abscesses, neutrophils in tubules | ==Thyroidization of tubules== (dilated tubules with ==eosinophilic hyaline casts==, colloid-like), interstitial chronic inflammation, ==periglomerular fibrosis== |
| Complications | ==Papillary necrosis (diabetics, obstruction, sickle cell, analgesics)==, pyonephrosis, perinephric abscess | ==Hypertension, CKD==, FSGS |

- ==Xanthogranulomatous pyelonephritis== — ==Proteus, staghorn calculi==, ==foamy macrophages (yellow nodules)==, mimics RCC.
- ==Emphysematous pyelonephritis== — gas-forming, diabetics.

:::mnemo Causes of renal papillary necrosis — "POSTCARDS"
==P==yelonephritis · ==O==bstruction · ==S==ickle cell · ==T==B · ==C==irrhosis · ==A==nalgesics (phenacetin, NSAIDs) · ==R==enal vein thrombosis · ==D==iabetes · ==S==ystemic vasculitis.
:::

### Acute (drug-induced) interstitial nephritis :

- Drugs : ==penicillins (methicillin), NSAIDs, PPIs, diuretics (thiazides), rifampicin, sulfonamides, allopurinol==; ==type I / IV hypersensitivity==, 2–40 days after exposure.
- ==Fever, rash, eosinophilia, eosinophiluria, sterile pyuria, hematuria==; interstitial edema + ==eosinophils, lymphocytes, granulomas==.
- ==Analgesic nephropathy== — ==papillary necrosis + chronic interstitial nephritis==; ↑ risk of ==urothelial carcinoma of renal pelvis==.

## Chronic Kidney Disease & ESRD

Causes (descending) :

1. ==Diabetic nephropathy== (most common)
2. ==Hypertensive nephrosclerosis==
3. Chronic glomerulonephritis
4. ADPKD, chronic pyelonephritis / reflux, obstructive uropathy
5. CKD of unknown etiology (==Uddanam, Andhra Pradesh== — agricultural workers)

- End-stage kidney : ==bilaterally contracted, granular (≤ 50 g)==; ==obsolescent glomeruli (hyalinized)==, interstitial fibrosis, ==thyroidization==, ==acquired cystic disease (dialysis → RCC risk ↑)==.

### Effects of uremia (systemic) :

| System | Manifestation |
|---|---|
| Fluid & electrolytes | Metabolic acidosis, hyperkalemia, ==hyperphosphatemia, hypocalcemia== |
| Bone | ==Renal osteodystrophy== — ==osteitis fibrosa cystica (secondary HPT)==, ==osteomalacia (↓ 1,25-D)==, adynamic bone disease; ==metastatic calcification== |
| Hematologic | ==Normocytic anemia (↓ EPO)==, ==bleeding (platelet dysfunction)== |
| CVS | ==Fibrinous (uremic) pericarditis==, HTN, CHF, accelerated atherosclerosis |
| GIT | Uremic gastroenteritis, ==fetor== |
| Neuromuscular | ==Peripheral neuropathy, encephalopathy, asterixis== |
| Skin | ==Uremic frost==, pruritus, ==calciphylaxis== |
| Lung | ==Uremic pneumonitis ("bat-wing" edema)== |

## Renal Tumours

### Renal cell carcinoma (RCC) :

- ==Most common primary malignant renal tumour in adults==; 6th–7th decade, M > F; arises from ==tubular epithelium==.
- Risk factors : ==tobacco (most important)==, obesity, HTN, unopposed estrogen, ==acquired cystic disease (dialysis)==, ==VHL==, cadmium, asbestos.
- Classic triad (10%) : ==hematuria (most common symptom), flank pain, palpable mass==.
- Paraneoplastic : ==polycythemia (EPO)==, hypercalcemia (PTHrP), HTN (renin), Cushing, feminization, amyloidosis, ==Stauffer syndrome (hepatic dysfunction without metastasis)==.
- Spread : ==renal vein invasion → IVC → right heart (tumour thrombus)==; ==hematogenous — lung (cannonball, most common), bone (lytic)==; left ==varicocele== (left renal vein obstruction).

| Type | Frequency | Genetics | Morphology |
|---|---|---|---|
| ==Clear cell RCC== | ==65–70%== | ==VHL loss (3p deletion)== → ↑ HIF → VEGF; ==sporadic & VHL syndrome== | ==Golden-yellow (lipid) solitary mass, hemorrhage, necrosis==; ==clear cells (glycogen + lipid) in nests with delicate branching vasculature (chicken-wire)==; ==CA-IX, PAX8, vimentin +== |
| ==Papillary RCC== | 10–15% | ==MET (type 1 — hereditary papillary RCC)==, ==trisomy 7, 17==, loss of Y; type 2 — ==FH (HLRCC)== | ==Multifocal, bilateral==; papillae with foamy macrophages, ==psammoma bodies==; dialysis-associated |
| ==Chromophobe RCC== | 5% | ==Hypodiploidy (multiple chromosome losses)==; ==Birt–Hogg–Dubé (folliculin)== | ==Pale eosinophilic cells with prominent cell membranes, perinuclear halo ("raisinoid")==; ==Hale colloidal iron +==, ==CK7 diffuse==, CD117 +; good prognosis |
| Collecting duct (Bellini) | < 1% | — | Medulla; aggressive |
| Renal medullary carcinoma | Rare | ==SMARCB1 (INI1) loss== | ==Sickle cell trait==, young |

:::mnemo RCC genetics — "3 words for 3 chromosomes"
==Clear cell → 3p (VHL)== — "clear has 3 letters + 2"; ==Papillary → 7 & 17 trisomy (MET)==; ==Chromophobe → loss of whole chromosomes (hypodiploid)==.
:::

### Wilms tumour (Nephroblastoma) :

- ==Most common primary renal tumour of childhood (2–5 years)==; ==bilateral in 5–10%==.
- ==Triphasic histology== : ==blastemal (small blue cells), stromal (fibrocytic/myxoid, skeletal muscle), epithelial (abortive tubules & glomeruli)==; ==anaplasia (TP53) → poor prognosis==.
- ==Nephrogenic rests== — precursor lesions.
- Presents as ==large abdominal mass not crossing midline==, hematuria, HTN.

| Syndrome | Gene | Features |
|---|---|---|
| ==WAGR== | ==WT1 deletion (11p13)== (+ PAX6) | ==W==ilms, ==A==niridia, ==G==enitourinary anomalies, mental ==R==etardation — 33% risk |
| ==Denys–Drash== | ==WT1 dominant-negative point mutation== | ==Gonadal dysgenesis (male pseudohermaphroditism), early nephropathy (diffuse mesangial sclerosis)== — 90% risk |
| ==Beckwith–Wiedemann== | ==WT2 (11p15.5) — IGF2 imprinting== | ==Organomegaly, macroglossia, hemihypertrophy, omphalocele==, hypoglycemia; also ==hepatoblastoma, adrenocortical tumours== |

:::pearl
- Wilms vs neuroblastoma : ==Wilms — intrinsic renal, does not cross midline, rarely calcified, smooth==; ==neuroblastoma — adrenal, crosses midline, calcified (85%), ↑ VMA/HVA, encases vessels==, < 2 years.
- ==Mesoblastic nephroma== — most common renal tumour in ==newborns / first 3 months== (ETV6-NTRK3 in cellular type).
:::

## Urinary Bladder & Urothelial Tract

### Cystitis :

| Type | Key features |
|---|---|
| Acute bacterial | ==E. coli (most common)==; women; catheter |
| ==Hemorrhagic cystitis== | ==Cyclophosphamide (acrolein — prevent with MESNA)==, adenovirus, radiation |
| ==Interstitial cystitis (Hunner ulcer)== | Middle-aged women; chronic pelvic pain; ==mast cells==; ==Hunner ulcers== |
| ==Malakoplakia== | ==Defective phagocytosis of E. coli==, immunosuppressed; soft yellow plaques; ==von Hansemann histiocytes + Michaelis–Gutmann bodies (laminated, calcified, PAS +, von Kossa +)== |
| Polypoid cystitis | Catheter irritation |
| ==Cystitis glandularis / cystica== | Brunn nests → cysts / glandular metaplasia; ==intestinal metaplasia → adenocarcinoma== (rare) |
| Schistosomal cystitis | ==S. haematobium== — calcified eggs, ==squamous metaplasia → SCC== |
| TB cystitis | Secondary to renal TB; ==sterile pyuria==, ==contracted "thimble" bladder==, golf-hole ureteric orifice |

### Bladder tumours :

- ==Urothelial (transitional cell) carcinoma — 90%==; men > 50 y.
- Risk factors : ==cigarette smoking (most important)==, ==aromatic amines (β-naphthylamine — dye, rubber, leather industries)==, ==cyclophosphamide==, ==phenacetin==, ==arsenic==, ==aristolochic acid (Balkan nephropathy, Chinese herbs — upper tract)==, ==Schistosoma (SCC)==, chronic irritation (catheter, stones).
- Two pathways :

| Pathway | Genetics | Lesion |
|---|---|---|
| ==Papillary (low-grade, non-invasive)== — 70% | ==FGFR3, HRAS==, 9p/9q deletion | ==Papilloma, PUNLMP, low-grade papillary carcinoma== — recur but rarely invade |
| ==Flat (carcinoma in situ) → invasive== | ==TP53, RB== | ==CIS (flat, high-grade)==, ==muscle-invasive carcinoma== |

- Presentation : ==painless hematuria (most common)==; ==field effect (multifocal, recurrence)==; ==intravesical BCG for CIS==.
- Grading WHO : papilloma → PUNLMP → low-grade → high-grade papillary urothelial carcinoma.
- Other : ==SCC (Schistosoma, Egypt)==, ==adenocarcinoma (urachal remnant — dome; exstrophy)==, ==botryoid rhabdomyosarcoma (children — grape-like)==.

## Urinary Tract Tuberculosis

- ==Hematogenous== spread from lung (often latent primary); ==cortex → papillae (ulcerocavernous lesions) → caseation → calyces → descending to ureter, bladder, epididymis, seminal vesicles, prostate==.
- Features : ==sterile pyuria (acid urine, WBCs, no growth on routine culture)==, hematuria, ==frequency (bladder contraction)==; ==putty (cement) kidney / autonephrectomy (calcified)==; ==ureteric strictures — "pipe-stem / beaded ureter"==; =="golf-hole" ureteric orifice==; ==thimble bladder==.
- Diagnosis : ==3 early-morning urine samples for AFB culture==, CBNAAT; IVU — moth-eaten calyx.

:::green Rank boosters — kidney
- ==Fibrillary GN== — ==DNAJB9 +==; randomly arranged fibrils 12–24 nm (Congo red negative).
- ==Collapsing glomerulopathy== — ==HIV, COVID-19 (APOL1)==, pamidronate.
- ==C1q nephropathy, IgM nephropathy== — FSGS-like.
- ==Anti-PLA2R== monitors therapy response in membranous nephropathy.
- ==Hypertension in children — most common cause : renal parenchymal disease (GN, reflux)==.
- ==Bartter (TAL — like loop diuretics), Gitelman (DCT — like thiazides; hypomagnesemia, hypocalciuria), Liddle (ENaC gain — HTN, hypokalemia, ↓ renin & aldosterone)==.
- ==Most common renal tumour in tuberous sclerosis : angiomyolipoma==; ==VHL — clear cell RCC (bilateral, multifocal)==.
:::
