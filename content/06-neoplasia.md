# Neoplasia

## Definitions & Nomenclature

- ==Neoplasia== : "new growth" — disorder of cell growth triggered by ==acquired (or inherited) mutations== affecting a single cell and its ==clonal progeny==; growth is ==autonomous== and persists after the stimulus is removed (Willis).
- Components : ==parenchyma== (neoplastic cells — determine behaviour and name) + ==stroma== (connective tissue, vessels — support). Abundant collagenous stroma = ==desmoplasia== → ==scirrhous== (stony hard) tumours, e.g. breast carcinoma.

| Tissue of origin | Benign | Malignant |
|---|---|---|
| Fibrous tissue | Fibroma | Fibrosarcoma |
| Fat | ==Lipoma== (most common soft tissue tumour) | ==Liposarcoma== (most common adult soft-tissue sarcoma along with UPS) |
| Cartilage | Chondroma | Chondrosarcoma |
| Bone | Osteoma | Osteosarcoma |
| Smooth muscle | ==Leiomyoma== | Leiomyosarcoma |
| Skeletal muscle | Rhabdomyoma | ==Rhabdomyosarcoma== (most common sarcoma of childhood) |
| Blood vessels | Hemangioma | Angiosarcoma |
| Squamous epithelium | Squamous papilloma | Squamous cell carcinoma |
| Glandular epithelium | ==Adenoma==, cystadenoma | ==Adenocarcinoma== |
| Melanocytes | Nevus | Melanoma |
| Hematopoietic | — | Leukemia, lymphoma |
| Germ cells | Mature teratoma | Immature teratoma, seminoma |

### Misnomers (malignant despite benign-sounding names) :

1. ==Lymphoma== 2. ==Melanoma== 3. ==Mesothelioma== 4. ==Seminoma== 5. Hepatoma (HCC) 6. Astrocytoma, glioma 7. Myeloma

### Tumour-like lesions :

| Term | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ==Hamartoma== | Disorganized mass of ==mature tissue indigenous to the site== | ==Pulmonary hamartoma== (cartilage — "popcorn calcification"), Peutz–Jeghers polyp |
| ==Choristoma (heterotopia)== | ==Normal tissue at an abnormal site== | ==Pancreatic rest in stomach wall==, Meckel's gastric mucosa |
| ==Teratoma== | From ==totipotent germ cells== — tissues of ==more than one germ layer== | Ovarian mature cystic teratoma (dermoid) |
| Mixed tumour | Divergent differentiation of ==one== germ layer | ==Pleomorphic adenoma== of salivary gland, fibroadenoma |
| Blastoma | Tumour of embryonic tissue | Retinoblastoma, nephroblastoma |

## Benign vs Malignant

| Feature | Benign | Malignant |
|---|---|---|
| Differentiation | ==Well differentiated==, resembles tissue of origin | Varies; ==anaplasia== in poorly differentiated |
| Rate of growth | Slow, may stop | Erratic, rapid; ↑ mitoses |
| Local invasion | ==Encapsulated==, expansile, cohesive | ==Locally invasive==, infiltrative, no capsule |
| Metastasis | ==Absent== | ==Frequently present — the single most definitive feature== |

### Anaplasia (lack of differentiation) — features :

1. ==Pleomorphism== — variation in size and shape of cells and nuclei
2. ==Abnormal nuclear morphology== — hyperchromasia, ==↑ N:C ratio (up to 1:1, normal 1:4–1:6)==, coarse chromatin, prominent nucleoli
3. ==Mitoses== — numerous, ==atypical (tripolar, quadripolar, multipolar spindles)==
4. ==Loss of polarity==
5. Tumour giant cells, ischemic necrosis

### Dysplasia :

- Disordered growth : ==pleomorphism, hyperchromasia, ↑ mitoses, loss of architectural orientation==.
- ==Carcinoma in situ== : dysplastic changes involve the ==full thickness of epithelium==, but ==basement membrane intact==.
- Dysplasia ≠ cancer; low-grade dysplasia may regress.

:::green Exceptions to "benign never metastasizes / malignant always metastasizes"
- Malignant tumours that rarely metastasize : ==basal cell carcinoma== of skin, ==gliomas (primary CNS tumours)==, ==verrucous carcinoma==, dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans.
- "Benign" tumours that may metastasize : ==pleomorphic adenoma== (metastasizing PA), ==giant cell tumour of bone== (lung), ==leiomyoma (benign metastasizing)==, ameloblastoma.
- ==Most common malignant tumour of liver, lung, brain, bone, adrenal = metastasis==.
:::

## Invasion & Metastasis

### Steps of invasion of ECM :

1. ==Loosening of tumour cells== — ==↓ E-cadherin== (cell–cell adhesion; CDH1 mutation in ==diffuse gastric cancer, lobular breast cancer==), ==epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT)== — SNAIL, TWIST
2. ==Local degradation of basement membrane / ECM== — ==MMP-2 & MMP-9 (type IV collagenases)==, cathepsin D, uPA
3. ==Changes in attachment to ECM== — integrins, laminin receptors
4. ==Migration (locomotion)== — autocrine motility factors (HGF/c-MET), cleavage products of ECM

### Routes of spread :

| Route | Typical tumours |
|---|---|
| ==Seeding of body cavities== | ==Ovarian carcinoma== (peritoneum), CNS tumours (CSF — medulloblastoma), ==pseudomyxoma peritonei (appendiceal mucinous)==, ==Krukenberg tumour== (gastric → ovary, transcoelomic/lymphatic) |
| ==Lymphatic spread== | ==Carcinomas== (most common route). ==Sentinel lymph node biopsy== — breast, melanoma |
| ==Hematogenous spread== | ==Sarcomas==; carcinomas : ==RCC (renal vein)==, ==HCC (portal / hepatic vein)==, ==follicular thyroid==, choriocarcinoma |

:::pearl
- ==Sarcomas spread via blood; carcinomas via lymphatics== — exceptions : ==carcinomas via blood : RCC, HCC, follicular Ca thyroid, choriocarcinoma==; ==sarcomas via lymph : rhabdomyosarcoma, synovial sarcoma, epithelioid sarcoma, clear cell sarcoma, angiosarcoma==.
- Most common sites of hematogenous metastasis : ==liver and lung==. ==Bone metastasis== : prostate (osteoblastic), breast (mixed), lung, kidney (lytic), thyroid (lytic).
- ==Virchow's node (left supraclavicular)== — gastric carcinoma; ==Sister Mary Joseph nodule== — umbilical metastasis.
- ==Seed and soil hypothesis== — Paget; ==CXCR4–CXCL12== guides breast cancer to bone / lung.
:::

## Molecular Basis of Cancer — Hallmarks

:::mnemo Hallmarks of cancer (Hanahan & Weinberg)
1. ==Self-sufficiency in growth signals== (oncogenes)
2. ==Insensitivity to growth-inhibitory signals== (tumour suppressors)
3. ==Altered cellular metabolism (Warburg effect)==
4. ==Evasion of apoptosis==
5. ==Limitless replicative potential (telomerase)==
6. ==Sustained angiogenesis==
7. ==Invasion and metastasis==
8. ==Evasion of immune surveillance==
— Enabling : ==genomic instability==, ==tumour-promoting inflammation==.
:::

:::flow RB and the cell cycle
Growth factors → ==cyclin D – CDK4/6==
==Hypophosphorylated RB== (active) binds E2F → ==blocks G1→S==
CDK4/6 phosphorylates RB → ==hyperphosphorylated RB releases E2F== → S-phase genes (cyclin E)
Inhibitors : ==p16 (INK4)==, ==p21, p27 (CIP/KIP)== ; HPV ==E7 binds RB==
:::

:::flow p53 response to DNA damage
DNA damage (radiation, UV, chemicals) → ==ATM / ATR== kinases activate p53 (releases from ==MDM2==)
==p21== → cell cycle arrest (G1) + DNA repair (GADD45) || ==PUMA, NOXA, BAX== → apoptosis || Senescence (miR-34)
Repair successful → cell resumes || Repair fails → apoptosis/senescence
HPV ==E6== degrades p53 ; ==MDM2 amplification== in sarcomas (liposarcoma)
:::

### DNA repair defects :

| Defect | Syndrome / tumour |
|---|---|
| ==Mismatch repair (MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, PMS2)== | ==Lynch syndrome (HNPCC)== — colon (right-sided), ==endometrium==, ovary; ==microsatellite instability== |
| ==Nucleotide excision repair== | ==Xeroderma pigmentosum== (UV — pyrimidine dimers) — skin SCC, BCC, melanoma |

| Base excision repair | ==MUTYH-associated polyposis== |

### Warburg effect, telomerase, angiogenesis :

- ==Warburg effect (aerobic glycolysis)== — cancer cells use glycolysis even with adequate oxygen; basis of ==FDG-PET==; driven by PI3K/AKT, MYC; ==IDH1/2 mutation → oncometabolite 2-hydroxyglutarate== (gliomas, AML, cholangiocarcinoma).
- ==Telomerase reactivation== in 85–95% cancers (TERT promoter mutations — melanoma, GBM).
- ==Angiogenic switch== — ==VEGF, bFGF==; inhibitors : ==angiostatin, endostatin, thrombospondin-1== (p53 induces). Drug : ==bevacizumab (anti-VEGF)==.

## Carcinogenesis

:::flow Multistep carcinogenesis (chemical)
==Initiation== — exposure to carcinogen → ==permanent, irreversible DNA damage (mutation)==; initiator alone is not sufficient
==Promotion== — promoters (==phorbol esters (TPA), hormones, phenols, drugs==) induce proliferation of initiated cells; ==reversible, non-mutagenic==
Progression → benign → malignant tumour; additional mutations (==tumour heterogeneity==)
:::

### Radiation carcinogenesis :

- ==UV-B== → ==pyrimidine (thymine) dimers== → skin cancer (SCC, BCC, melanoma); xeroderma pigmentosum.
- Ionizing radiation → ==leukemias (most common; AML, CML — except CLL)==, ==papillary thyroid carcinoma (children)==, breast, lung; latent period ==leukemia 7–12 years; solid tumours decades==.

## Laboratory Diagnosis of Cancer

1. ==Histologic examination== — ==excision / incision / core biopsy==; ==frozen section== (intra-operative, quick — margins, nature of lesion)
2. ==Cytology== — ==FNAC== (breast, thyroid, lymph nodes, salivary); exfoliative (==Pap smear==, sputum, urine, effusions)
3. ==Immunohistochemistry (IHC)== — identify cell of origin, prognostic/predictive markers
4. ==Flow cytometry== — leukemias, lymphomas (CD markers), DNA ploidy
5. ==Molecular diagnosis== — PCR (BCR-ABL monitoring), ==FISH (HER2, N-MYC, translocations)==, NGS, ==liquid biopsy (ctDNA)==
6. ==Tumour markers== (serum)
