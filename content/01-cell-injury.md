# Cell Injury, Adaptation & Cell Death

## Overview

:::flow
Normal cell (homeostasis)
Stress / increased demand
Adaptation (hypertrophy, hyperplasia, atrophy, metaplasia) || Injurious stimulus beyond adaptive capacity
Reversible injury (swelling, fatty change) || Severe / persistent injury
Cell death : ==Necrosis== or ==Apoptosis==
:::

## Cellular Adaptations

| Adaptation | Definition | Physiological example | Pathological example |
|---|---|---|---|
| ==Hypertrophy== | ↑ cell **size** → ↑ organ size | Uterus in pregnancy (also hyperplasia), skeletal muscle in body-builders | ==LVH in hypertension==, aortic stenosis |
| ==Hyperplasia== | ↑ cell **number** (only in dividing cells) | Breast at puberty / lactation, liver regeneration after partial hepatectomy | ==Endometrial hyperplasia== (unopposed estrogen), BPH, warts (HPV) |
| ==Atrophy== | ↓ cell size and number | Thymus after puberty, uterus after delivery | Disuse, denervation, ↓ blood supply, pressure, ↓ nutrition, ↓ endocrine |
| ==Metaplasia== | One adult differentiated cell type replaced by another | — | ==Barrett esophagus==, squamous metaplasia in smokers' bronchi |
| Hypoplasia | Incomplete development of an organ (fewer cells) | — | Unilateral renal hypoplasia |
| Agenesis / Aplasia | Complete absence of organ / its primordium; aplasia = primordium present but no development | — | Renal agenesis (Potter sequence) |
| Dysplasia | Disordered growth: loss of uniformity and architectural orientation | — | CIN, Barrett with dysplasia |

### Hypertrophy :

- Seen in ==permanent cells== (cardiac, skeletal muscle) which cannot divide.
- Mechanism : mechanical sensors + growth factors (TGF-β, IGF-1, FGF) + vasoactive agents (α-adrenergic agonists, endothelin-1, angiotensin II).
- Signal pathways : ==PI3K/AKT== (physiological), ==G-protein coupled receptor== signalling (pathological).
- Switch of contractile proteins to fetal forms : ==α-myosin heavy chain → β-MHC== (slower, energy-efficient contraction).
- ↑ ==ANP== (atrial natriuretic peptide) gene expression in hypertrophied ventricle.

:::green
- Pure hypertrophy example : ==skeletal muscle==, heart. Pure hyperplasia : ==liver regeneration==. Both together : ==gravid uterus== (smooth muscle).
- Selective hypertrophy of an organelle : ↑ ==smooth ER== in hepatocytes with ==barbiturates / phenobarbital== (CYP450 induction).
- Cardiac myocyte hypertrophy limit → myofibril fragmentation, loss of contractile elements → ==cardiac failure==.
:::

### Hyperplasia :

- Occurs only in tissues capable of division (==labile and stable cells==).
- Driven by growth factors and stem-cell recruitment.
- Pathological hyperplasia is a fertile soil for cancer : ==endometrial hyperplasia → endometrial carcinoma==; ==HPV papillomas==.
- ==BPH is NOT pre-malignant== for prostatic carcinoma.

### Atrophy :

Causes :

1. Decreased workload (disuse atrophy)
2. Loss of innervation (denervation atrophy)
3. Diminished blood supply (senile atrophy of brain)
4. Inadequate nutrition (marasmus, cachexia — ==TNF-α==)
5. Loss of endocrine stimulation (endometrium after menopause)
6. Pressure (erosion of vertebra by aortic aneurysm)

Mechanism :

- ↓ protein synthesis + ↑ protein degradation via ==ubiquitin–proteasome pathway==.
- ==Autophagy== (self-eating) → autophagic vacuoles.
- Residual bodies contain ==lipofuscin== → ==brown atrophy== (heart, liver of elderly).

### Metaplasia :

- Reversible; ==reprogramming of stem cells==, not change of differentiated cells.
- Driven by cytokines/growth factors + retinoic acid; transcription factors (==SOX, CDX2==).

| Type | Example | Cause |
|---|---|---|
| Columnar → squamous (most common) | Bronchus | ==Cigarette smoking==, ==vitamin A deficiency== |
| Columnar → squamous | Endocervix (transformation zone) | Low vaginal pH |
| Transitional → squamous | Urinary bladder | ==Schistosoma haematobium==, stones |
| Squamous → columnar (intestinal) | ==Barrett esophagus== | GERD (acid reflux) |
| Gastric → intestinal | Stomach | ==H. pylori==, autoimmune gastritis |
| Connective tissue | ==Myositis ossificans== (bone in muscle) | Trauma / hemorrhage |

:::pearl
- ==Epithelial metaplasia is reversible; dysplasia may be reversible (low grade); carcinoma in situ is not invasive but irreversible.==
- ==Myositis ossificans== : "zonal pattern" — mature bone at periphery, immature fibroblasts at centre (opposite of osteosarcoma).
- ==Apocrine metaplasia== in fibrocystic breast — not pre-malignant.
:::

## Cell Injury — Causes

1. ==Oxygen deprivation== (hypoxia, ischemia) — the most common cause.
2. Physical agents (trauma, temperature, radiation, electric shock).
3. Chemical agents and drugs.
4. Infectious agents.
5. Immunologic reactions.
6. Genetic derangements.
7. Nutritional imbalances.

:::green Hypoxia vs ischemia
- ==Ischemia injures tissues faster than hypoxia== — loss of both O2 and metabolic substrates (glucose) + accumulation of toxic metabolites.
- In hypoxia, ==anaerobic glycolysis continues==; in ischemia it stops once substrates are exhausted.
:::

### Susceptibility of cells to ischemia :

| Cell | Time to irreversible injury |
|---|---|
| ==Neurons== | ==3–5 min== |
| Myocardium | ==20–30 min== (irreversible); hepatocytes 1–2 h |
| Fibroblasts, skeletal muscle | Many hours |

- Most vulnerable neurons : ==CA1 of hippocampus (Sommer sector)== → Purkinje cells of cerebellum → pyramidal cells of neocortex (layers 3, 5, 6).
- Most sensitive renal segment : ==proximal tubule (S3 segment)== and ==thick ascending limb==.
- Liver : ==zone 3 (centrilobular)== most vulnerable to ischemia.

## Mechanisms of Cell Injury

:::tree
Mechanisms of cell injury
  ↓ ATP (mitochondrial damage)
    ↓ Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase → cell swelling
    ↑ anaerobic glycolysis → ↓ pH, ↓ glycogen
    Ribosome detachment → ↓ protein synthesis
    Unfolded protein response
  Mitochondrial damage
    ==MPTP== opening (cyclophilin D)
    Leakage of ==cytochrome c== → apoptosis
  ↑ Cytosolic Ca²⁺
    Activates phospholipases, proteases, endonucleases, ATPases
    Opens MPTP
  ROS (free radicals)
    Lipid peroxidation of membranes
    Protein oxidation, DNA damage
  Membrane damage
    Plasma membrane → loss of osmotic balance
    Lysosomal membrane → enzyme leakage
:::

### Free radicals (ROS) :

Generation :

1. Normal metabolism (reduction–oxidation reactions)
2. Absorption of radiant energy (UV, X-rays → hydrolysis of water)
3. Leukocyte respiratory burst (==NADPH oxidase==)
4. Enzymatic metabolism of chemicals (CCl₄ → CCl₃•)
5. Transition metals : ==Fenton reaction== (Fe²⁺ + H₂O₂ → Fe³⁺ + OH• + OH⁻)
6. Nitric oxide (NO) → peroxynitrite (ONOO⁻)

Removal (antioxidants) :

1. Antioxidants : ==vitamin E== (lipid-soluble, membrane), vitamin A, ascorbic acid, glutathione.
2. Storage/transport proteins that bind free iron and copper : transferrin, ferritin, lactoferrin, ceruloplasmin.
3. Enzymes :
    - ==Superoxide dismutase== : O₂⁻ → H₂O₂
    - ==Catalase== (peroxisomes) : 2H₂O₂ → O₂ + 2H₂O
    - ==Glutathione peroxidase== : H₂O₂ + 2GSH → GSSG + 2H₂O

:::pearl
- Most reactive (damaging) free radical : ==hydroxyl radical (OH•)==.
- ==Superoxide dismutase is mitochondrial (Mn-SOD) and cytosolic (Cu/Zn-SOD)==.
- Free-radical injury examples : ==CCl₄ hepatotoxicity==, ==reperfusion injury==, ==radiation==, ==oxygen toxicity (retinopathy of prematurity, BPD)==, ==acetaminophen (NAPQI)==, aging.
:::

### Ischemia–reperfusion injury :

1. ↑ ROS generation by damaged mitochondria and leukocytes.
2. ==Intracellular calcium overload== (MPTP opening).
3. Inflammation : neutrophil influx with cytokines.
4. ==Complement activation== (IgM deposits in ischemic tissue).

- Morphology in heart : ==contraction band necrosis== (hyper-contracted myofibrils due to Ca²⁺ influx).

### Chemical injury :

| Direct acting | Indirect (converted to toxic metabolites by CYP450) |
|---|---|
| ==Mercuric chloride== — binds SH groups (kidney, GI) | ==CCl₄== → CCl₃• — fatty liver, centrilobular necrosis |
| Cyanide — inhibits ==cytochrome oxidase== | ==Acetaminophen== → NAPQI — zone 3 necrosis, ↓ glutathione |
| Chemotherapeutic agents | Bromobenzene, vinyl chloride |

## Reversible Cell Injury

Two main features :

1. ==Cellular swelling (hydropic change / vacuolar degeneration)== — earliest change; failure of Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase.
2. ==Fatty change (steatosis)== — liver, heart, kidney (organs in lipid metabolism).

### Ultrastructural changes (reversible) :

1. Plasma membrane alterations : ==blebbing==, blunting, loss of microvilli.
2. Mitochondrial changes : ==swelling==, ==small amorphous densities==.
3. ==Dilatation of ER== with detachment of polysomes.
4. Nuclear alterations : disaggregation of granular and fibrillar elements.
5. ==Myelin figures== : phospholipid whorls from damaged membranes.

:::green Reversible vs irreversible — point of no return
- Two phenomena characterize irreversibility : (1) ==inability to restore mitochondrial function== (lack of oxidative phosphorylation) even after reperfusion, (2) ==profound disturbances in membrane function==.
- ==Large flocculent amorphous densities in mitochondria== → ==irreversible== (ischemic myocardium).
- ==Leakage of enzymes== (troponin, CK-MB, ALT) indicates membrane damage → irreversible injury.
:::

### Fatty change (steatosis) — liver :

Causes :

1. ==Alcohol== (most common in developed countries)
2. Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (obesity, diabetes, metabolic syndrome)
3. Protein malnutrition (==kwashiorkor== — ↓ apoprotein)
4. Toxins (CCl₄), drugs (tetracycline, valproate)
5. Starvation, hypoxia, pregnancy (acute fatty liver of pregnancy)
6. Reye syndrome (aspirin in children, microvesicular)

:::flow Pathogenesis of fatty liver
Free fatty acids from adipose / diet || ↑ hepatic synthesis (alcohol: ↑ NADH)
FFA → ==Triglycerides== in hepatocyte
Apoprotein deficiency (malnutrition, CCl₄) blocks lipoprotein formation
TG accumulate → ==fatty liver==
:::

| Feature | Microvesicular | Macrovesicular |
|---|---|---|
| Appearance | Many small droplets, ==central nucleus== | Single large vacuole, ==nucleus pushed to periphery== |
| Causes | ==Reye syndrome==, acute fatty liver of pregnancy, valproate, tetracycline | Alcohol, obesity, diabetes, malnutrition |
| Significance | Mitochondrial dysfunction; can be severe | Usually benign, reversible |

- Stains : fat dissolved in routine paraffin sections (clear vacuoles); demonstrated by ==Oil Red O== and ==Sudan III/IV== on ==frozen sections==; osmium tetroxide (black).
- Heart : fatty change in prolonged moderate hypoxia (profound anemia) → ==tigered effect / "thrush-breast" heart== (bands of yellow myocardium alternating with red–brown) in the papillary muscles.

### Hyaline change :

- Homogeneous, glassy, pink (eosinophilic) appearance on H&E — a descriptive term, not a specific lesion.

| Intracellular hyaline | Extracellular hyaline |
|---|---|
| ==Mallory–Denk bodies== (alcoholic hepatitis — keratin) | ==Hyaline arteriolosclerosis== (benign HTN, diabetes) |
| ==Russell bodies== (immunoglobulin in plasma cells) | Hyaline in old scars, corpora albicantia |
| ==Councilman bodies== (apoptotic hepatocytes) | Hyaline membranes (ARDS, HMD of newborn) |
| Hyaline droplets in PCT (proteinuria) | Amyloid (hyaline-like) |
| Zenker's hyaline degeneration of muscle (typhoid) | Hyaline cartilage-like scar |

### Other intracellular accumulations :

| Substance | Examples |
|---|---|
| Cholesterol | Atherosclerosis (foam cells), ==xanthomas==, ==cholesterolosis== (strawberry gallbladder), Niemann-Pick type C |
| Proteins | Russell bodies, Mallory hyaline, α1-antitrypsin globules (==PAS +, diastase-resistant==) |
| Glycogen | Diabetes (==Armanni–Ebstein lesion== — PCT/loop of Henle), glycogen storage diseases (stain : ==PAS + diastase-sensitive==, Best carmine) |
| Pigments | Lipofuscin, melanin, hemosiderin, bilirubin, carbon (see Pigments chapter) |

## Irreversible Injury — Necrosis

- ==Necrosis== : death of cells in a living organism due to severe injury, always ==pathological==, with ==inflammation==.

### Morphology of necrotic cell :

- Cytoplasm : ↑ ==eosinophilia== (loss of RNA + denatured proteins), glassy, ==myelin figures==, calcification.
- Nucleus :

:::hflow
==Pyknosis== (shrinkage, ↑ basophilia) > ==Karyorrhexis== (fragmentation) > ==Karyolysis== (fading; DNase)
:::

### Patterns of necrosis :

| Type | Mechanism / features | Classic examples |
|---|---|---|
| ==Coagulative== | Protein denaturation > enzymatic digestion; ==architecture preserved for days== ("ghost cells") | ==Infarcts of all solid organs except brain== (heart, kidney, spleen) |
| ==Liquefactive== | Enzymatic digestion dominates; tissue becomes liquid, creamy (pus) | ==Brain infarct==, abscess (bacterial/fungal) |
| ==Caseous== | "Cheese-like", friable white; ==granuloma with central amorphous granular debris== | ==Tuberculosis==, histoplasmosis, coccidioidomycosis |
| ==Fat necrosis== | Lipases release FA → combine with Ca²⁺ (==saponification==) → chalky white | ==Acute pancreatitis==, traumatic fat necrosis of breast |
| ==Fibrinoid== | Immune complexes + fibrin in vessel walls; bright pink, amorphous | ==Malignant hypertension==, vasculitis (PAN), ==Aschoff body==, preeclampsia |
| Gangrenous (clinical term) | Usually coagulative necrosis of limb (dry), + bacterial liquefaction (wet) | Diabetic foot, intestinal gangrene |

:::green Extra high-yield
- ==Gumma== (tertiary syphilis) — rubbery necrosis, ==architecture of vessels partially preserved (ghost outlines)== — vs caseation where outlines are lost.
- Caseation without TB : ==histoplasmosis, coccidioidomycosis, sarcoid (rarely, no), tularemia, cat-scratch (stellate microabscess)==.
- ==Fibrinoid necrosis in placenta== (normal finding at term — Nitabuch layer).
- Fat necrosis in breast can mimic carcinoma clinically (hard lump + skin retraction) — history of trauma.
:::

## Apoptosis

- Programmed cell death; ==membrane intact==, ==no inflammation==.
- Hallmark morphology : ==cell shrinkage, chromatin condensation (pyknosis, most characteristic)==, cytoplasmic blebs, ==apoptotic bodies==, phagocytosis by macrophages.
- DNA fragmentation : ==step-ladder pattern== on agarose gel (internucleosomal, 180–200 bp multiples) vs ==smear pattern== in necrosis.

### Causes :

Physiological :

1. Embryogenesis (implantation, organogenesis, involution — e.g., interdigital webs)
2. Hormone-dependent involution (endometrium in menstruation, ovarian follicular atresia, breast after weaning)
3. Cell turnover in proliferating populations (intestinal crypt epithelium)
4. Elimination of ==self-reactive lymphocytes== (negative selection in thymus)
5. Death of host cells that have served their purpose (neutrophils after acute inflammation)

Pathological :

1. DNA damage (radiation, cytotoxic drugs)
2. Accumulation of misfolded proteins (ER stress)
3. Infections — viral hepatitis (==Councilman bodies==)
4. Pathological atrophy in parenchymal organs after duct obstruction (pancreas, kidney, parotid)
5. Cell death by cytotoxic T cells (graft rejection)

### Pathways :

:::cols
#### Intrinsic (mitochondrial) pathway

- ==Most common== pathway.
- Trigger : ↓ growth factors, DNA damage (==p53==), misfolded proteins.
- BH3-only sensors (==BIM, BID, BAD, PUMA, NOXA==) ↑ → activate ==BAX/BAK== (pro-apoptotic).
- ↓ ==BCL-2, BCL-XL, MCL-1== (anti-apoptotic).
- ==Cytochrome c== release → binds ==APAF-1== → ==apoptosome== → ==caspase-9== (initiator).
---
#### Extrinsic (death receptor) pathway

- Death receptors : ==TNFR1, Fas (CD95)== — have ==death domain==.
- ==FasL== (on activated T cells) binds Fas → adaptor ==FADD== → ==caspase-8== (also caspase-10).
- Inhibitor : ==FLIP== (viral and cellular).
- Role : eliminating self-reactive lymphocytes, CTL killing.
- Defect → ==autoimmune lymphoproliferative syndrome (ALPS)==.
:::

:::flow Execution phase
Initiator caspases : ==caspase-9== (intrinsic) || ==caspase-8, -10== (extrinsic)
Executioner caspases : ==caspase-3==, -6, -7
Activate DNase (CAD), break cytoskeleton, nuclear matrix
Apoptotic bodies + "eat-me" signal : ==phosphatidylserine flips to outer leaflet== (binds ==annexin V==)
Phagocytosis by macrophages — no inflammation
:::

:::green Rank boosters — apoptosis
- ==Caspase = cysteine protease that cleaves after aspartic acid==.
- ==BCL-2 overexpression== by t(14;18) → ==follicular lymphoma== (failure of apoptosis).
- ==IAP (inhibitor of apoptosis proteins)== block caspases; ==SMAC/DIABLO== from mitochondria neutralize IAP.
- Markers of apoptosis : ==annexin V==, ==TUNEL assay==, caspase-3 cleavage, DNA ladder.
- ==Granzyme B== from CTL activates caspases directly (and BID); ==perforin== makes pores.
- ==p53 → PUMA/NOXA → BAX/BAK==; also arrests cell cycle via p21.
:::

### Necrosis vs Apoptosis :

| Feature | Necrosis | Apoptosis |
|---|---|---|
| Cell size | ==Enlarged (swelling)== | ==Reduced (shrinkage)== |
| Nucleus | Pyknosis → karyorrhexis → karyolysis | Fragmentation into nucleosome-size pieces |
| Plasma membrane | Disrupted | ==Intact==; altered structure (lipid orientation) |
| Cellular contents | Enzymatic digestion; may leak out | Intact; released in apoptotic bodies |
| Inflammation | ==Frequent== | ==None== |
| Role | Always pathological | Often physiological; may be pathological |
| DNA on gel | Smear | ==Ladder== |
| ATP | Not required | ==Energy-dependent== |

## Other Forms of Cell Death

| Type | Key molecules | Features / examples |
|---|---|---|
| ==Necroptosis== | ==RIPK1, RIPK3 → MLKL== | Programmed necrosis, caspase-independent; TNF; ==Crohn disease==, pancreatitis, reperfusion; ==CMV inhibits== |
| ==Pyroptosis== | ==Inflammasome → caspase-1== (also 4, 5, 11) → ==gasdermin D== pores | Fever (IL-1β, IL-18); infected cells (Salmonella, Shigella) |
| ==Ferroptosis== | Iron-dependent ==lipid peroxidation==; ↓ ==GPX4 / glutathione== | Stroke, neurodegeneration, cancer; inhibited by ==ferrostatin== |
| ==Autophagy== | ==Beclin-1, LC3, ATG== genes; mTOR inhibition | Starvation survival; defects in ==Crohn (ATG16L1)==, Alzheimer, Huntington |
| Entosis | Cell engulfed by neighbour | "Cell-in-cell" in tumours |
| Anoikis | Loss of integrin–matrix attachment | Prevents metastasis; resistance in cancer |
| NETosis | Neutrophil extracellular traps (PAD4) | Sepsis, SLE, thrombosis |
| Parthanatos | ==PARP-1== overactivation, AIF | Stroke, DNA damage |

:::mnemo Ferroptosis
"==Fe==rro = ==Fe==, peroxidation, ==GPX4== guards" — iron + lipid ROS; ==not apoptosis (no caspases)==.
:::

## Autophagy

- "Self-eating" — delivers cytoplasmic materials to lysosomes.
- Types : ==macroautophagy== (main), microautophagy, ==chaperone-mediated autophagy==.
- Steps : nucleation (==Beclin-1 / VPS34==) → elongation (==ATG genes, LC3==) → autophagosome → fusion with lysosome → autophagolysosome.
- Induced by nutrient deprivation (==mTOR inhibition==).

## Cellular Aging

1. ==DNA damage== (accumulated mutations; defective repair — ==Werner syndrome: DNA helicase==, Bloom syndrome).
2. ==Replicative senescence== — telomere shortening (==Hayflick limit==); telomerase active in germ/stem/cancer cells.
3. Defective protein homeostasis (↓ chaperones, ↓ proteasome).
4. Dysregulated nutrient sensing : ==caloric restriction ↑ lifespan== via ↓ IGF-1 and ↓ mTOR, ↑ ==sirtuins== (NAD-dependent deacetylases).
5. Persistent inflammation ("inflammaging"), ROS.

:::green Progeria syndromes
| Syndrome | Defect |
|---|---|
| ==Werner syndrome== | ==WRN — DNA helicase (RecQ)== — adult-onset premature aging |
| ==Hutchinson–Gilford progeria== | ==LMNA (lamin A)== — progerin — childhood aging |
| Bloom syndrome | BLM helicase |
| Ataxia telangiectasia | ATM kinase — radiation sensitivity |
| Dyskeratosis congenita | Telomerase (TERC/TERT/DKC1) |
:::
