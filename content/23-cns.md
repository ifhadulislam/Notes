# Central Nervous System, Nerve & Muscle

## Cells & Reaction Patterns

| Cell | Reaction to injury |
|---|---|
| ==Neuron== | ==Acute neuronal injury ("red neurons") — 12–24 h after hypoxia : shrinkage, eosinophilic cytoplasm, pyknosis, loss of Nissl substance== ; ==axonal reaction — central chromatolysis (swollen cell body, Nissl dispersed to periphery, eccentric nucleus)==; neuronal inclusions (Lewy, Negri, Pick, tangles); ==Wallerian degeneration== |
| ==Astrocyte== | ==Gliosis — most important indicator of CNS injury (no fibroblasts)==; ==gemistocytes (reactive — abundant pink cytoplasm, GFAP +)==; ==Rosenthal fibres (pilocytic astrocytoma, Alexander disease — GFAP mutation)==; ==corpora amylacea (aging)==; ==Alzheimer type II astrocytes (hyperammonemia — liver failure, Wilson)== |
| ==Microglia== | ==CNS macrophages== — ==rod cells (neurosyphilis)==, ==microglial nodules (viral encephalitis)==, ==neuronophagia== |
| ==Oligodendrocyte== | Myelin; ==viral inclusions in PML (JC virus)==; glial cytoplasmic inclusions (MSA — α-synuclein) |
| Ependyma | Granular ependymitis (CMV) |

## Cerebral Edema, Raised ICP & Herniation

| Type | Mechanism | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| ==Vasogenic== | ==Disruption of blood–brain barrier → ↑ extracellular fluid (white matter)== | ==Tumours, abscess, trauma, inflammation== — responds to steroids |
| ==Cytotoxic== | ==Cell membrane injury → intracellular fluid (grey & white matter)== | ==Ischemia / hypoxia (early), toxic, metabolic== |
| Interstitial (hydrocephalic) | Transependymal CSF flow | ==Hydrocephalus — periventricular== |

- Gross : ==flattened gyri, narrowed sulci, compressed ventricles==.

### Hydrocephalus :

- ==Non-communicating (obstructive)== — obstruction within ventricular system (==aqueductal stenosis — most common congenital==, colloid cyst of 3rd ventricle, tumours, Arnold–Chiari).
- ==Communicating== — impaired CSF resorption by ==arachnoid granulations (post-meningitis, subarachnoid hemorrhage)==.
- ==Normal pressure hydrocephalus== — elderly : =="wet, wobbly, wacky" — urinary incontinence, gait apraxia, dementia==.
- ==Hydrocephalus ex vacuo== — compensatory ventricular enlargement due to brain atrophy (Alzheimer).

### Herniation :

| Type | Structure | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| ==Subfalcine (cingulate)== | Cingulate gyrus under falx | ==Compression of anterior cerebral artery → infarct== |
| ==Transtentorial (uncal)== | ==Uncus of temporal lobe over tentorium== | ==CN III compression (ipsilateral dilated pupil — "blown pupil", ptosis, down & out)==, ==PCA compression (occipital infarct)==, ==Kernohan notch (contralateral cerebral peduncle → ipsilateral hemiparesis — false localizing)==, ==Duret hemorrhages (midbrain & pons — stretching of paramedian arteries) — fatal== |
| ==Tonsillar== | ==Cerebellar tonsils through foramen magnum== | ==Brainstem compression → respiratory & cardiac arrest (fatal)== ; ==never do LP with raised ICP== |

## Infections of the CNS

### Meningitis — organisms by age :

| Age | Organisms |
|---|---|
| ==Neonates (< 1 month)== | ==Group B Streptococcus (S. agalactiae), E. coli, Listeria monocytogenes== |
| Children (1 month – 6 y) | ==S. pneumoniae, N. meningitidis==, H. influenzae b (unvaccinated) |
| ==Adolescents & young adults== | ==N. meningitidis (epidemics — petechial rash, Waterhouse–Friderichsen)== |
| ==Adults & elderly== | ==S. pneumoniae (most common)==, N. meningitidis, ==Listeria (> 60 y, immunocompromised)== |
| Post-neurosurgery / shunt | Staph. epidermidis, S. aureus, Gram-negatives |

- ==Acute pyogenic meningitis== — ==purulent exudate in leptomeninges (subarachnoid space)==; ==pneumococcal — over convexities (thick exudate)==; ==H. influenzae — basal==; ==meningococcal — vertex==; complications : ==ventriculitis, hydrocephalus (communicating), arachnoid fibrosis, cerebritis, venous sinus thrombosis, deafness (children), SIADH==.
- ==Tuberculous meningitis== — ==basal meninges — gelatinous / fibrinous exudate obliterating cisterns==, ==cranial nerve palsies (VI, III)==, ==obliterative endarteritis (Heubner arteritis) → infarcts (basal ganglia)==, ==communicating hydrocephalus==; ==Rich focus (subpial / subependymal tubercle) ruptures into subarachnoid space==; ==tuberculoma (intracranial mass — ring-enhancing; most common intracranial mass lesion in India along with NCC)==.

### Brain abscess :

- ==Sources== : ==direct spread (otitis media / mastoiditis → temporal lobe & cerebellum; frontal / ethmoid sinusitis → frontal lobe)==, ==hematogenous (lung abscess, bronchiectasis, IE — multiple, MCA territory)==, ==cyanotic congenital heart disease (R→L shunt — TOF)==, trauma.
- Organisms : ==Streptococci (most common), Staph, anaerobes==; ==Toxoplasma & Nocardia (immunocompromised)==.
- Morphology : ==central liquefactive necrosis, surrounding fibrous capsule (fibroblasts from vessels), reactive gliosis with gemistocytes, edema== → ==ring-enhancing lesion==.

| Virus | Key pathology |
|---|---|
| ==HSV-1== | ==Most common sporadic fatal encephalitis==; ==hemorrhagic necrotizing — inferior & medial temporal lobes, orbital gyri==; Cowdry A inclusions ; ==PCR CSF== ; acyclovir |
| ==Japanese encephalitis== | ==India (Gorakhpur, Assam) — Culex mosquito, pigs (amplifier), herons==; ==thalamus, basal ganglia, substantia nigra== |
| ==Rabies== | ==Negri bodies (Purkinje, hippocampal pyramidal)==; ==minimal inflammation==; hydrophobia |
| ==Polio== | ==Anterior horn cells (lower motor neuron), medullary — bulbar== |

| ==Measles — SSPE== | ==Years after measles (< 2 y infection)==; ==↑↑ anti-measles antibodies in CSF==, inclusions in neurons & oligodendrocytes, ==periodic EEG complexes== |
| CMV | Periventricular, ependymitis (AIDS / congenital) |
| Arboviruses | Perivascular cuffing, microglial nodules, ==neuronophagia== |

### Prion diseases :

- ==Misfolded PrPˢᶜ (β-sheet) induces conformational change in PrPᶜ (α-helix)== ; ==protease-resistant==; ==transmissible==; ==no inflammation==.
- ==Spongiform change (vacuoles in neuropil & neurons — grey matter)==, ==neuronal loss, gliosis==, ==kuru plaques (amyloid — vCJD — "florid plaques")==.
- ==Sporadic CJD (most common, 85%) — rapidly progressive dementia + myoclonus, periodic sharp-wave EEG, ↑ 14-3-3 protein & RT-QuIC (CSF)==; ==variant CJD (BSE — young, psychiatric, pulvinar sign)==; ==familial (PRNP), iatrogenic (corneal transplant, growth hormone, dura)==; ==fatal familial insomnia (thalamus)==, GSS, kuru (cannibalism — Fore tribe).

## Cerebrovascular Disease

### Hypoxia / ischemia :

- ==Global ischemia (cardiac arrest, shock)== : most vulnerable — ==CA1 hippocampus (Sommer sector), Purkinje cells, pyramidal neurons of neocortex (layers 3, 5, 6) → laminar necrosis==; ==watershed (border-zone) infarcts — between ACA & MCA territories ("parasagittal")==.
- ==Focal ischemia — infarction== :

| Time | Changes |
|---|---|
| ==First 6–12 h== | Little change |
| ==12–48 h== | ==Red (eosinophilic) neurons==, edema, ==neutrophils (24 h)== |
| ==2–3 days onward== | ==Macrophages (gitter cells — lipid-laden "foamy")== ; ==liquefactive necrosis== |
| ==Weeks – months== | ==Cyst (cavity) surrounded by reactive gliosis== |

- ==Thrombotic infarcts== — ==atherosclerosis (carotid bifurcation, MCA, basilar)== — pale (anemic).
- ==Embolic infarcts== — ==cardiac mural thrombi (AF, MI), valve disease, carotid plaques==; ==MCA (most common)==; ==hemorrhagic (red) infarcts — reperfusion of damaged vessels==.
- ==Lacunar infarcts== — ==small (< 15 mm), deep (lenticular nucleus, thalamus, internal capsule, pons)==; ==hypertension — hyaline arteriolosclerosis / lipohyalinosis of penetrating arteries==; pure motor / pure sensory stroke.

| Type | Cause | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| ==Hypertensive intracerebral hemorrhage== | ==Charcot–Bouchard microaneurysms / arteriolosclerosis — lenticulostriate arteries== | ==Basal ganglia — putamen (most common, 50–60%)==, thalamus, pons, cerebellum |
| ==Cerebral amyloid angiopathy== | ==Aβ amyloid in leptomeningeal & cortical vessels== | ==Lobar hemorrhages in elderly (recurrent)==, Alzheimer; ==ApoE ε2, ε4== |

| ==Cavernous malformation== | ==Back-to-back dilated vessels without intervening brain== | ==CCM1/2/3==; cerebellum, pons |
| Venous angioma | Most common vascular malformation, benign | — |

## Demyelinating Diseases

### Multiple sclerosis (MS) :

- ==Autoimmune (CD4 Th1 & Th17) demyelination== ; ==women 20–40 y, higher latitudes==; ==HLA-DRB1*1501 (DR2), IL-2R, IL-7R==; ==EBV, low vitamin D==, smoking.
- ==Relapsing–remitting (most common)==, primary progressive, secondary progressive.
- Morphology : ==plaques — well-circumscribed, grey, glassy lesions in white matter — periventricular (Dawson fingers — perivenular), corpus callosum, optic nerves & chiasm, brainstem, spinal cord==; ==active plaques — demyelination with lipid-laden macrophages & perivascular lymphocytes; axons relatively preserved==; inactive — gliosis; ==shadow plaques (remyelination)==.
- Clinical : ==optic neuritis (often first), internuclear ophthalmoplegia (MLF — bilateral in young = MS), Lhermitte sign, Uhthoff phenomenon (heat worsens), Charcot triad (nystagmus, intention tremor, scanning speech)==, sensory, bladder; ==McDonald criteria (dissemination in space & time)==; ==MRI (T2 / FLAIR)==, ==CSF oligoclonal bands==.

| Other demyelinating disorders | Key features |
|---|---|
| ==Neuromyelitis optica (Devic)== | ==Anti-aquaporin-4 (astrocyte)== ; ==severe optic neuritis + longitudinally extensive transverse myelitis (≥ 3 segments)==; Asian women |
| ==MOG antibody disease== | Children; ADEM-like |
| ==Acute disseminated encephalomyelitis (ADEM)== | ==Post-viral / post-vaccination (monophasic)==; ==perivenular demyelination of same age== |
| ==Acute hemorrhagic leukoencephalitis (Hurst)== | Fulminant ADEM, young |
| ==Central pontine myelinolysis (osmotic demyelination)== | ==Rapid correction of hyponatremia==; ==central pons — "locked-in"== |
| ==Leukodystrophies== | ==Metachromatic (arylsulfatase A), Krabbe (galactocerebrosidase — globoid cells), X-linked adrenoleukodystrophy (ABCD1 — VLCFA ↑ + adrenal insufficiency)== |

## CNS Tumours

- ==Children : 2nd most common malignancy (after leukemia) & most common solid tumour==; ==mostly infratentorial (posterior fossa)==; adults — ==supratentorial==.
- ==Metastases — most common CNS tumours in adults== : ==lung > breast > melanoma > kidney > GIT==; ==grey–white junction, multiple, well-circumscribed==; ==hemorrhagic metastases — melanoma, choriocarcinoma, RCC, thyroid==.

## Peripheral Nerve

| Pattern | Pathology | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| ==Axonal neuropathy== | ==Wallerian-like degeneration, axon loss== | ==Diabetes (distal symmetric)==, alcohol, toxins (vincristine, arsenic), ==uremia==, ==B12/B1 deficiency== |

- ==Traumatic (amputation) neuroma== — disorganized axon regrowth; ==Morton neuroma (interdigital, 3rd–4th toes)==.

- ==Most common primary brain tumour in adults : meningioma (overall); most common primary malignant : glioblastoma; children : pilocytic astrocytoma (overall), medulloblastoma (malignant)==.
- ==CNS tumours rarely metastasize outside the CNS== (except medulloblastoma via shunt).
- ==Diffuse midline glioma, H3 K27-altered== — children's pontine glioma (DIPG); worst prognosis.
- ==Colloid cyst of 3rd ventricle== — foramen of Monro obstruction → ==positional headache, sudden death==.
- ==Wernicke encephalopathy — mammillary bodies, periaqueductal grey (hemorrhage & necrosis)==; ==subacute combined degeneration — posterior & lateral columns (B12)==; ==tabes dorsalis — posterior columns & dorsal roots (syphilis)==; ==syringomyelia — cape-like loss of pain & temperature (crossing fibres), Chiari I==.
- ==Neural tube defects — ↑ maternal serum AFP & acetylcholinesterase (amniotic)==; ==folic acid 400 µg (4 mg if previous NTD) prevents==; ==Arnold–Chiari II — myelomeningocele + hydrocephalus==; ==Dandy–Walker — cystic 4th ventricle, vermis agenesis==.
