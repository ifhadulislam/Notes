# Calcification & Amyloidosis

## Pathological Calcification

- Abnormal tissue deposition of ==calcium salts== with small amounts of iron, magnesium and other minerals.
- Stain : ==von Kossa== (black — actually stains phosphate/carbonate), ==Alizarin red S== (red–orange, calcium). On H&E : ==basophilic==, amorphous, granular.

| Feature | ==Dystrophic== | ==Metastatic== |
|---|---|---|
| Serum calcium | ==Normal== | ==Hypercalcemia== |
| Tissue | ==Dead / dying (necrotic) tissue== | ==Normal (viable) tissue== |
| Common sites | Atheroma, damaged / aging heart valves, caseous TB, infarcts, dead parasites | ==Interstitial tissue of gastric mucosa, kidneys, lungs, systemic arteries, pulmonary veins== |
| Mechanism | Initiation in membrane-bound vesicles (phospholipids + phosphatase) or extracellular | Loss of acid (H⁺) → alkaline compartment favours deposition |
| Clinical | Usually a marker of prior injury | ==Nephrocalcinosis==, respiratory deficits |

:::mnemo Sites of metastatic calcification
"Calcium ==lo==ves ==alk==aline tissues" — tissues that ==excrete acid== have an internal alkaline compartment : ==gastric mucosa (HCl), kidney (H⁺), lung (CO₂)==.
:::

### Causes of metastatic calcification (hypercalcemia) :

1. ↑ ==Parathyroid hormone== — primary hyperparathyroidism, ==PTHrP from tumours== (squamous cell lung carcinoma)
2. ==Bone destruction== — multiple myeloma, leukemia, skeletal metastases, Paget disease (accelerated turnover), immobilization
3. ==Vitamin D-related== — vitamin D intoxication, ==sarcoidosis== (macrophages activate vitamin D), idiopathic hypercalcemia of infancy (Williams syndrome)
4. ==Renal failure== — phosphate retention → secondary hyperparathyroidism
5. Milk-alkali syndrome (excess calcium + absorbable antacids)
6. Aluminium intoxication (chronic dialysis)

### Dystrophic calcification — examples :

1. ==Atherosclerotic plaques== (advanced atheroma)
2. ==Aging or damaged heart valves== (calcific aortic stenosis, ==bicuspid aortic valve==)
3. ==Caseous necrosis in TB== (Ghon complex, calcified lymph nodes)
4. Fat necrosis (pancreatitis — "chalky white")
5. ==Mönckeberg medial calcific sclerosis== (medium muscular arteries, > 50 y)
6. Dead parasites — hydatid cyst wall, cysticercus, Schistosoma eggs
7. Thrombi (==phleboliths== in veins)
8. Tumours — ==psammoma bodies==

### Psammoma bodies :

- Laminated, concentric calcified structures (==dystrophic==) formed by progressive deposition on single necrotic cells.

:::mnemo Psammoma bodies — "PSaMMOMa"
==P==apillary carcinoma of thyroid · ==S==erous cystadenocarcinoma of ovary · ==M==eningioma · ==M==alignant ==M==esothelioma · also prolactinoma, ==papillary RCC==, somatostatinoma (duodenum), endometrial serous carcinoma.
:::

:::green
- ==Calciphylaxis (calcific uremic arteriolopathy)== — end-stage renal disease, ↑ Ca × P product, medial calcification of small arteries → painful skin necrosis.
- ==Calcinosis cutis== in ==CREST / limited scleroderma==, dermatomyositis (children).
- Heterotopic ossification = bone formation (not just calcium) in soft tissue — myositis ossificans.
- ==Michaelis–Gutmann bodies== (target-like, calcified, PAS+, von Kossa +) — ==malakoplakia== of bladder (defective macrophage lysosomal killing of E. coli).
- ==Schaumann bodies== (laminated calcium + protein) and ==asteroid bodies== — sarcoidosis.
:::

## Amyloidosis

- Extracellular deposition of ==misfolded proteins== which aggregate into ==insoluble fibrils== with a ==β-pleated sheet== configuration.
- All amyloid : ~95% fibril protein + 5% ==P component== (serum amyloid P, ==pentraxin==) + glycosaminoglycans (==heparan sulfate==).

### Physical nature :

- EM : ==non-branching fibrils, 7.5–10 nm diameter==, indefinite length.
- X-ray crystallography / infrared spectroscopy : ==cross-β-pleated sheet== conformation (responsible for Congo red birefringence).

### Staining :

| Stain | Result |
|---|---|
| H&E | Pale ==eosinophilic, amorphous, hyaline== extracellular material |
| ==Congo red== (light microscopy) | Pink / red (==salmon-pink==) |
| Congo red under ==polarized light== | ==Apple-green birefringence== (most specific) |
| Gross (Lugol's iodine) | ==Mahogany brown== → blue-violet with dilute H₂SO₄ (starch-like, hence "amyloid" by Virchow) |
| Thioflavin T / S | Fluorescence (UV) |
| ==Crystal violet / methyl violet== | ==Metachromasia== (rose-pink on violet) |
| Immunohistochemistry / ==mass spectrometry== | ==Typing of amyloid== (gold standard for type) |

### Classification of amyloid proteins :

| Protein | Precursor | Clinical setting |
|---|---|---|
| ==AL (amyloid light chain)== | Ig light chains (==λ > κ==) | ==Primary / plasma cell dyscrasia== — multiple myeloma, B-cell neoplasms |
| ==AA (amyloid-associated)== | ==SAA== (acute phase, liver; IL-1/IL-6) | ==Reactive systemic (secondary)== — chronic inflammation |
| ==Aβ2m (β2-microglobulin)== | β2-microglobulin (MHC-I) | ==Long-term hemodialysis== — carpal tunnel, joints |
| ==ATTR== | ==Transthyretin== (normal or mutant) | ==Familial amyloid polyneuropathy==; ==senile systemic amyloidosis== (heart, wild-type TTR) |
| ==Aβ (amyloid β)== | ==APP== (amyloid precursor protein) | ==Alzheimer disease==, cerebral amyloid angiopathy |
| ==ACal== | (Pro)calcitonin | ==Medullary carcinoma thyroid== |
| ==AIAPP== | ==Amylin== (islet amyloid polypeptide) | ==Type 2 diabetes== (islets) |
| AANF | Atrial natriuretic factor | Isolated atrial amyloidosis (elderly) |
| APrP | Prion protein | Spongiform encephalopathies |
| AA (mutant pyrin) | SAA | ==Familial Mediterranean fever (MEFV — pyrin)== |
| ALys / AFib / AApoA1 | Lysozyme, fibrinogen, apo A1 | Hereditary renal amyloidosis |

### Causes of reactive systemic (AA) amyloidosis :

1. ==Tuberculosis== (most common in India), bronchiectasis, chronic osteomyelitis, lepromatous leprosy
2. ==Rheumatoid arthritis== (most common in West), ankylosing spondylitis
3. Inflammatory bowel disease (==Crohn > UC==)
4. Skin popping (s.c. heroin abuse)
5. Non-immunocyte tumours : ==renal cell carcinoma==, ==Hodgkin lymphoma==
6. Familial Mediterranean fever, other auto-inflammatory syndromes

### Pathogenesis :

:::flow
Normal protein that misfolds (↑ production or ↑ tendency) || Mutant protein prone to misfolding
Misfolded proteins escape ==proteasomal / chaperone degradation==
Aggregation into oligomers → fibrils (==β-pleated sheet==)
Extracellular deposition → ==pressure atrophy== of adjacent cells; oligomers are ==toxic==
:::

:::pearl
- ==Primary AL amyloid : most common form in developed countries==; occurs in ==5–15% of multiple myeloma==.
- ==Bence Jones protein== (free light chains) in urine/serum in AL amyloidosis — even without overt myeloma ("monoclonal gammopathy").
- ==AL amyloidosis = Congo red + ; AA = Congo red loses affinity after potassium permanganate (KMnO₄) treatment; AL is permanganate-resistant==.
:::

### Morphology (organ involvement) :

| Organ | Features |
|---|---|
| ==Kidney== (most common, most serious) | Enlarged, pale, waxy; deposits in ==glomeruli (mesangium, subendothelial)== → ==nephrotic syndrome==; later shrunken. Peritubular and vessel walls. |
| ==Spleen== | ==Sago spleen== (deposits in ==splenic follicles== — tapioca-like granules); ==Lardaceous spleen== (deposits in ==red pulp / sinusoid walls== — map-like). |
| Liver | Hepatomegaly; deposits in ==space of Disse== → pressure atrophy of hepatocytes; liver function usually preserved. |
| ==Heart== | ==Restrictive cardiomyopathy==, conduction disturbances, ==low voltage ECG with thick walls==; "sparkling / granular" echo; ==most common cause of death in AL amyloid==. Subendocardial deposits. |
| Others | ==Macroglossia== (AL), ==carpal tunnel syndrome==, adrenal insufficiency, GI malabsorption, ==periorbital purpura ("raccoon eyes", AL)==, ==pinch purpura== |

### Diagnosis — biopsy sites :

1. ==Abdominal fat pad aspiration== — preferred screening (simple, safe)
2. ==Rectal biopsy== (submucosal vessels) — 75% positive
3. Gingival / labial salivary gland biopsy
4. ==Renal biopsy== — most sensitive in renal disease
5. Bone marrow biopsy (AL)
6. Serum / urine immunofixation and ==free light chain assay==; scintigraphy (==SAP scan==, ==Tc-PYP for ATTR cardiac==)

:::green Rank boosters
- ==Localized amyloidosis== : ==nodular lung, larynx, skin, bladder, tongue==; ==endocrine amyloid== (medullary Ca thyroid, islet in T2DM, pituitary prolactinoma).
- ==Senile cardiac amyloidosis (wild-type ATTR)== — men > 70 y; ==ATTR V122I== mutation in 4% African Americans.
- Drug for ATTR : ==Tafamidis== (TTR stabilizer), ==Patisiran== (siRNA), Inotersen.
- β2-microglobulin is ==not removed by cuprophane dialysis membranes==.
- Amyloid in ==Alzheimer== : ==Aβ42== — neuritic plaques; ==APP gene on chromosome 21== → early Alzheimer in Down syndrome.
- Hereditary : ==Familial Mediterranean fever== — ==autosomal recessive, MEFV (pyrin), AA amyloid==; ==Muckle–Wells== (cryopyrin) — AA.
- Most common cause of death in systemic amyloidosis overall : renal failure (AA) / cardiac (AL).
:::
